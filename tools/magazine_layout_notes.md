# Magazine layout notes for the Florida Gulf Coast Guide (weekly)
Researched Oct 22, 2026 (free web sources). Applies to our navy/gold, Cinzel + Georgia/Gelasio system.

## What to borrow
1. **One dominant hero image per page.** Feature openers in food and travel magazines (Bon Appétit, Condé Nast Traveler) build around one hero shot, with smaller supporting images. *Apply:* each feature page gets one large photo slot about 45-55% of the width, plus one or two clearly smaller ones. Never three equal photos. [Visual Journal Craft](https://visualjournalcraft.com/article/magazine-spread-design-tutorial); [FlipLink](https://fliplink.me/blog/magazine-layouts)
2. **Mixed photo sizes on a fixed grid.** Wallpaper* City Guides use a tight 8-unit grid with 4 picture depths and 3 widths to vary rhythm while staying orderly. *Apply:* a 3-column grid; photos span 1, 2 or 3 columns at set heights (about 1.3, 2.2 and 3.3 in). [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/12-october-2006/a-tale-of-two-city-guides/)
3. **Asymmetry with alignment.** Off-center, hero-right/text-left layouts read as editorial; they stay calm only if every edge snaps to the grid. *Apply:* alternate hero side page to page (right on Social Minute, left on Community Focus). [FlipLink](https://fliplink.me/blog/magazine-layouts)
4. **Kicker, headline, deck.** Hierarchy runs kicker, headline, deck, then body; the deck bridges headline and image. *Apply:* small gold Cinzel kicker ("SOCIAL MINUTE"), white Cinzel headline, gold italic serif deck line. [Magazine Training eBook](https://magazinetraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/eBook-Intro-Design.pdf)
5. **Drop cap on the lede.** A 2-4 line initial marks the story's entry point. *Apply:* gold Cinzel drop cap on the first paragraph of feature pages only (Community Focus, Social Minute), not on data pages. [Typography Master](https://www.typographymaster.com/guide/editorial-typography)
6. **Pull quotes from real text, used sparingly.** Quote real copy at 1.5-2x body, aligned to the grid, about one per page. *Apply:* gold Cinzel or italic Gelasio pull quote between gold hairlines; never invented quotes or unverified claims. [Typography Master](https://www.typographymaster.com/guide/editorial-typography)
7. **Caption every photo.** Captions are among the most-read elements. *Apply:* small gold small-caps label under each photo slot (what it shows, and later the credit, e.g. "Photo: Guy Golan"). [Magazine Training eBook](https://magazinetraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/eBook-Intro-Design.pdf)
8. **White space as hierarchy.** Cereal and Kinfolk build hierarchy with negative space instead of many type sizes; Cereal's guides refuse to "cram every inch". *Apply:* fewer boxes on feature pages, more navy breathing room; keep gold outlines for practical info (hours, prices). [Studio Faculty: Cereal](https://www.studiofaculty.com/work/cereal-magazine); [Cereal City Guides](https://www.studiofaculty.com/work/cereal-city-guides)
9. **Consistent placement of practical info.** Cereal's city guides put venue address and details in the same spot every time. *Apply:* Social Minute's address/hours/phone always in a gold box in the same position each week. [Cereal City Guides](https://www.studiofaculty.com/work/cereal-city-guides)
10. **Pace dense and airy pages.** Alternate dense text/data pages with lighter image-led ones. *Apply:* Market Update and Insurance stay dense; Community Focus and Social Minute become airy, photo-led breathers between them. [FlipLink](https://fliplink.me/blog/magazine-layouts)
11. **Section signals.** Kinfolk used colored divider spreads; Cereal uses page color to separate venues from essays. *Apply:* keep navy everywhere, but give feature pages a gold kicker and drop cap so readers feel the shift from data to lifestyle. [Kinfolk study, Ghent Univ.](https://openjournals.ugent.be/jeps/article/71456/galley/195681/download/)
12. **Restraint.** Luxury travel editorial (Condé Nast Traveler, Monocle) relies on restrained serif type and little visual noise. *Apply:* no new colors, icons or effects; gold stays an accent. [designmd.app](https://designmd.app/library/editorial-de-viagem-sofisticado)

Note: Time Out, Garden & Gun, Saveur and Food & Wine layouts weren't analyzed from primary sources here; the points above come from the cited design write-ups.

## The guiding star: one fixed, deliberate position (added Oct 22, 2026)
**What National Geographic does**
- Its identity is built on one fixed device: the yellow border, which sits in the same place on every cover. Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv built the brand mark directly from that border. [C&G&H](https://cghnyc.com/work/project/national-geographic)
- The licensee style guide fixes the Yellow Icon's proportions ("may not be altered") and requires clear space of at least half the icon's height on every side, free of text and images. It limits the icon mostly to covers and product borders, says it "is not a graphic element", and says never to re-create it. [Nat Geo Style Guide for Licensees](https://media.journoportfolio.com/users/366908/uploads/605e2380-f07a-4a00-a876-651c60acc6f4.pdf)
- Nat Geo set fixed dimensions for the icon and rules for framing, so the mark stays recognizable across every medium. [CaseStudies.com / Brandfolder](https://www.casestudies.com/company/brandfolder-inc/case-study/evolving-one-of-the-worlds-most-iconic-recognizable-brands-national-geographic)
- Its staff describe the brand as "there from the beginning", not "something we apply at the end". [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/national-geographic-societys-rich-heritage-guides-through-new-territory-56303/)

**How we apply it**
1. **One position.** On every interior page the gold star sits at top center, set into a break in the outer gold frame line. It is never moved, scaled, recolored or repeated elsewhere on the page.
2. **Clear space.** The frame stops on either side of the star, and nothing else sits in the band above the kicker or title. This is our version of Nat Geo's area of isolation.
3. **Fixed proportions.** The size and gold color stay the same on every page: one star glyph, #D4AF37.
4. **It's a signature, not decoration.** No extra stars as bullets or ornaments. The only other star is the one built into the real logo's wordmark.
5. **Cover.** The cover's star is the one inside the real wordmark, under the arch, and it is never redrawn. The cover carries no frame star, so the logo's star is the only one on that page, the same way Nat Geo's cover is its own mark.

## Frame rule (Guy) (added Oct 22, 2026)
- **Everything stays on the plate.** Every photo and element stays inside the gold page frame, with at least the normal content margin (0.6 in from the page edge, the same as the text margins) between it and the inner frame line.
- **No bleeds.** Nothing may bleed to, touch or cross the frame or the page edge. This revises the earlier advice: any bleed suggested above (including the v6/v7 photos run out to the inner frame on pages 5 and 10, and "break the grid" via a bleeding photo in the FlipLink notes) is **not allowed** in this guide.
- **Overlap is still fine.** Stacking or overlapping photos inside the frame is allowed, e.g. the cocktail photo over the hero on Social Minute, or the clubhouse over the aerial on Community Focus, kept with a thin navy gap.
- Applied in v8: page 5's streets aerial was pulled in from the left frame, and page 10's restaurant photo from the right frame. The rest of the guide was checked and no photo was near the frame.
